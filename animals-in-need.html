Foster Network · In development

Helping the right support reach the right animal.

Happy’s is developing a Houston-based coordination and matching network for animals in need. The aim is to connect finders, fosters, animal-welfare organizations, veterinary professionals, transport support, adopters, and other community resources without replacing their work or requiring one person to solve every part of a case alone.

We are currently building the network, speaking with Houston animal-welfare professionals, and developing the operating standards that will guide future cases.

Interested in HelpingHow the Foster Network Works

Information. Connection. Support.

01Information

Help people understand which next step fits the situation and find appropriate existing resources.

A reviewed Houston directory and guidance from qualified professionals can clarify whether the next step may involve veterinary care, an owner search, community-cat/TNVR resources, rescue support, or safely leaving an animal in place while options are explored.

02Connection

Match a reviewed case with people or organizations whose verified capabilities and availability fit the need.

A structured needs profile can be compared with current capacity for fostering, veterinary care, transport, supplies, rescue expertise, or adoption support. Sensitive information would be shared only through a responsible, human-reviewed handoff.

03Support

Reduce the practical burden after someone says yes through clearer coordination, communication, and follow-up.

Clear commitments, reminders, status checks, and—where feasible in the future—coordination around supplies, transport, or professional referrals can keep one foster or finder from carrying every part of a case alone.

Building in Houston

We’re looking for the people who already know where the system breaks.

Before launching the Foster Network, Happy’s is speaking with Houston veterinarians, foster organizations, rescue groups, community-cat/TNVR organizations, animal-service providers, experienced fosters, and people who care about animals but have chosen not to foster.

Early research points to recurring pressure around capacity, foster follow-through, communication, scheduling, and administrative work. These conversations will determine which problems Happy’s should address, which responsibilities should remain with existing specialists, and where coordination could genuinely help.

What Happy’s is doing now

Houston directory

Organizing animal-welfare organizations and resources by service area, geography, and publicly available capabilities.

02

Foster-barrier research

03

Exploring how student organizations and university communities could contribute research, outreach, education, and supervised support.

Where things stand today

The Happy’s Foster Network is currently in development. Happy’s does not presently operate an animal shelter, provide veterinary care, guarantee foster or adoption placement, or offer emergency-response services through this page. Future programs, partnerships, screening standards, financial assistance, transportation services, and placement procedures remain subject to operational, legal, veterinary, insurance, and other professional review before launch.

If an animal appears to be experiencing an immediate medical emergency, contact a licensed veterinarian or appropriate emergency animal-care provider.
