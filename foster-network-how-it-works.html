How the Foster Network Works

Coordinate help around the animal.

Happy’s is exploring a distributed support model in which one person does not have to solve every part of an animal’s situation alone. The goal is to help connect each need with the people, professionals, organizations, or resources best equipped to address it.

The operating model is still being developed and will evolve through professional input, partner conversations, and real world pilot cases.

A guiding principle

Move resources when we can.Move animals only when necessary.

Repeatedly moving an animal can create additional stress and disruption. Happy’s is exploring a model that brings appropriate resources, information, services, and support to an animal’s existing safe environment whenever practical, while recognizing that some situations will require veterinary care, rescue transfer, fostering, or another appropriate placement.

Support for responsible next steps toward reunion, rescue transfer, or permanent placement when legally appropriate.

Which services are ultimately offered directly, coordinated through partners, or referred elsewhere will be determined as Happy’s develops its operating model and professional partnerships.

Help shape the network.

Happy’s is speaking with fosters, veterinarians, rescue organizations, community-cat groups, animal-service providers, and people who care about animals but have chosen not to foster. Those conversations will help determine what Happy’s should coordinate, what should remain with existing specialists, and where better support could genuinely help more animals.

The proposed flow

A coordinated case, not just a listing.

Imagine a kitten is found near an apartment complex.

  1. A finder submits basic information about the kitten and its current situation.

  2. The situation is reviewed to determine whether the animal may need urgent care, owner-search steps, community-cat guidance, fostering, or another appropriate resource.

  3. The case may involve several different needs rather than one single placement.

  4. Appropriate people, professionals, or organizations may be connected to the parts of the case they are equipped to handle.

  5. The case status can continue to be followed toward an appropriate outcome such as reunification, rescue transfer, fostering, or adoption.

The long-term idea is that a foster may provide the home, a veterinary professional may provide medical care, another person may help with transportation or supplies, and an animal-welfare organization may provide specialized expertise. Happy’s is exploring how to coordinate those pieces without expecting one finder or foster to carry the entire burden alone.
