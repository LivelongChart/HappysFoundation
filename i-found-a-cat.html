Houston guidance

I found an animal. What happens next?

The right next step depends on the species, immediate safety, health, location, and whether the animal may already have a home.

Species matter

Do not use one response for every animal.

01

Urgent situations

Keep your distance from an injured, aggressive, or distressed animal. For a dog or cat with major trauma, breathing trouble, collapse, seizures, or uncontrolled bleeding, call a licensed emergency veterinarian for instructions.

02

Cats and kittens

Look for identification, an eartip, nearby caregivers, and signs that a mother cat may be returning. Healthy community cats should not be relocated simply because they live outdoors.

03

Dogs

Contain the dog only if it can be done safely. Look for identification, arrange a microchip scan, and contact the appropriate municipal shelter or animal-services agency for the area where the dog was found.

04

Wildlife

Keep people and pets away, minimize handling, and do not offer food or medication. Contact a permitted wildlife rehabilitator or the appropriate local animal-services agency for species-specific guidance.

01

Assess the situation

Safety and urgent care come first.

Keep people and other animals at a safe distance. Avoid handling an injured, frightened, aggressive, or unfamiliar animal when doing so could cause injury. Trouble breathing, uncontrolled bleeding, seizures, collapse, major trauma, or an inability to stand or move normally requires urgent veterinary guidance.

Record where the animal was found. Note visible injuries or unusual behavior, and take a photograph from a safe distance if possible. When calling a veterinarian or animal-services organization, explain the animal’s condition, location, and the help needed. Ask for instructions before transporting the animal, especially when handling may be dangerous. For a sick or injured stray within Houston city limits, call BARC first to confirm its current procedure, hours, and jurisdiction.

Medical note

This is general triage information, not a diagnosis or veterinary treatment. Never give human medication. A licensed veterinary professional should determine care.

02

Cat and kitten guidance

Owned, lost, or at home outdoors?

Look for identification

Check for a collar or tag without putting yourself at risk. A veterinary clinic or shelter may be able to scan a safely contained, socialized cat for a microchip.

Ask nearby

Photograph the cat, ask neighbors, check local lost-pet posts, and post a found notice without publishing an exact home address.

Notice behavior and condition

A clean, healthy cat may be an indoor-outdoor pet or a cared-for community cat. An eartip usually indicates a community cat has already been sterilized through TNR.

Do not assume kittens are abandoned

A mother may be nearby and waiting for people to leave. Unless kittens are cold, injured, or in immediate danger, observe from a distance and seek age-appropriate guidance.

Do not remove or relocate a healthy community cat simply because the cat lives outdoors. Contact a local TNR/TNVR group for guidance when sterilization or community-cat support is needed.

03 / Choose the right type of help

Different organizations solve different parts of the problem.

No single Houston organization provides every service. Start with the animal’s species, condition, location, and likely status, then contact the resource designed for that need.

04

Confirm the next step

Confirm BeforeBringing anAnimal In

Before taking an animal to a shelter, rescue, or veterinary clinic, contact the organization to confirm its service area, current intake availability, hours, fees, and whether an appointment is required.

Explain where the animal was found, its general condition, and what assistance is needed. Some organizations can provide guidance or referrals even when they cannot accept an animal directly.

Organizations listed in Happy’s Houston directory operate independently. Inclusion does not indicate a formal partnership or guarantee that assistance is available.
