Emergency guidance

Injured, struggling to breathe, or in immediate danger?

Keep a safe distance. For serious injuries, breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, or uncontrolled bleeding, seek urgent veterinary guidance and call ahead for safe handling and transport instructions.

Assess the situation

Safety and urgent care come first.

Private emergency veterinary care and municipal animal-control assistance serve different purposes.

Find emergency and urgent resources

Warning signs requiring urgent veterinary guidance

  • Trouble breathing
  • Heavy or uncontrolled bleeding
  • Seizures or collapse
  • Major trauma
  • Inability to stand or move normally

What to do

  1. Keep people and pets at a safe distance.
  2. Avoid handling an injured, aggressive, frightened, or unfamiliar animal when doing so may be dangerous.
  3. Record the location and take a photograph from a safe distance if possible.
  4. Contact a veterinarian or the responsible animal-services authority for instructions before handling or transport.
Medical note

This is general guidance, not a diagnosis or veterinary treatment. Never give human medication to an animal. A licensed veterinary professional should determine medical care.

Cat and kitten guidance

Owned, lost, or at home outdoors?

Additional cat guidance — Alley Cat Allies ↗

Look for identification

Check for a collar or tag without putting yourself at risk. Many clinics and shelters offer microchip scans; confirm availability before visiting. Do not grab or restrain a frightened cat.

Ask nearby

Ask residents, check neighborhood lost-pet posts, and create a found report without publishing an exact address. Verify ownership with photos, records, or identifying features.

Notice behavior and condition

A healthy, well-groomed outdoor cat may have a home or caregiver. An eartip usually indicates sterilization through TNR; it does not mean the cat is abandoned.

Do not assume kittens are abandoned

A mother may be nearby. Observe healthy, warm, quiet kittens from a distance. Cold, injured, weak, lethargic, continuously distressed, or endangered kittens need prompt qualified guidance.

Do not remove or relocate a healthy community cat simply because the cat lives outdoors. Contact a local TNR/TNVR group for guidance when sterilization or community-cat support is needed.

Additional cat and kitten tips

If bitten or scratched, wash the area promptly and seek appropriate medical advice about infection and rabies exposure. For owner searches, consider Petco Love Lost ↗, Nextdoor, and relevant community groups.

Do not give kittens cow’s milk. Very young kittens can require specialized, frequent care. Review Alley Cat Allies’ kitten guidance ↗ before intervening.

02

Dog guidance

Lost, stray, or separated from home?

Found-dog guidance — Houston SPCA ↗

Look for identification

Check for a collar, identification tag, or other owner information without putting yourself at risk. Do not chase or forcibly restrain a frightened or aggressive dog.

Search nearby

Ask nearby residents, check neighborhood lost-pet posts, and report the dog through relevant lost-and-found services. Avoid publishing an exact residential address.

Arrange a microchip scan

Veterinary clinics and animal shelters may scan safely contained dogs for microchips. Contact the organization first to confirm availability and handling requirements.

Contact the responsible agency

Report the found dog to the appropriate municipal or county animal-services authority based on where the dog was found. Houston BARC and Harris County Pets serve different jurisdictions.

Do not assume a dog is abandoned simply because it is loose. An owner may be searching nearby. Verify ownership before transferring the animal to someone claiming it.

Wildlife guidance

Wild, injured, or simply passing through?

Find Harris County wildlife rehabilitators ↗

Directory listing does not guarantee immediate availability.

Keep your distance

Avoid unnecessary handling. Keep children and pets away from unfamiliar wildlife, particularly animals that are injured, frightened, or behaving unusually.

Observe before intervening

Young birds, rabbits, fawns, and other wildlife may be left alone temporarily while their parents remain nearby. Do not assume they are orphaned.

Recognize urgent situations

Visible injuries, severe weakness, breathing difficulty, entanglement, or immediate danger may require assistance from a qualified wildlife professional.

Contact a wildlife rehabilitator

Seek a properly permitted rehabilitator experienced with the specific species. Do not attempt to treat, feed, or relocate wildlife without appropriate guidance.

Do not handle bats, raccoons, skunks, or other potentially dangerous wildlife. Following a bite, scratch, or possible bat exposure, promptly contact a healthcare professional or public health authority.

Confirm the next step

Confirm before bringing an animal in.

Before visiting a shelter, rescue, or veterinary clinic, contact the organization to confirm that it can help with your situation.

  • Does it serve the area where the animal was found?
  • Does it accept this type of animal?
  • Is intake currently available?
  • What are its hours, fees, and appointment requirements?
  • Are there specific handling or transport instructions?

Organizations may offer advice or referrals even when they cannot accept an animal. Organizations listed in Happy’s Houston directory operate independently. Inclusion does not indicate a formal partnership or guarantee that assistance is available.
